###### **Global oceans are darkening, disrupting life beneath the surface: Study** 


![](./images/all_books_mdx/gen-mor-71st-edition/images/GEN-MOR_71ST_EDITION.pdf-0018-05.png)


<!-- Start of picture text -->

**A map focused on the UK and North Atlantic Ocean showing changes identified through this project. While the red markings show areas darkening, blue shows areas lightening.** 

A new study by the University of Plymouth reveals that over 21% of the global ocean has darkened between 2003 and 2022 due to shrinking photic zones—the sunlit layers crucial for marine life and photosynthesis. This darkening, caused by factors like rising sea temperatures, altered plankton dynamics, ocean circulation changes, and coastal runoff, is most prominent in climate-sensitive regions such as the Arctic, Antarctic, and Gulf Stream. As light penetration decreases, marine species that depend on sunlight for feeding, movement, and reproduction are being forced into shallower waters, increasing competition and threatening biodiversity.
