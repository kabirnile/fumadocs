###### **MoSJE launches app to empower waste pickers under the NAMASTE Scheme.** 





In a landmark move toward environmental justice and worker dignity, the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment launched the Waste Picker Enumeration App under the NAMASTE Scheme in New Delhi. This digital initiative aims to formally recognize and uplift 2.5 lakh waste pickers— unsung heroes of India’s circular economy—by providing them with occupational ID cards, health insurance, safety gear, skill training, and access to financial support. By empowering these workers and strengthening their collectives to manage 750 dry waste collection centers, the scheme ensures safer conditions and long-term livelihood security. Accompanying the launch were two key knowledge products that spotlight the social and economic realities of India’s sanitation workforce and offer guidance to integrate waste picker collectives into formal service delivery.

### **PVC: Biography and Story** <mark>ee</mark> 

**Subedar Major and Honorary Captain Yogendra Singh Yadav is one of India's most celebrated military heroes, renowned for his extraordinary bravery during the Kargil War in 1999. He is the youngest recipient of the Param Vir Chakra (PVC), India's highest wartime gallantry decoration, which he received at the age of 19.** 

###### **Early Life and Joining the Army:** 

**Yogendra Singh Yadav was born on May 10, 1980, in Aurangabad Ahir, Bulandshahr District, Uttar Pradesh. He came from a military family; his father, Karan Singh Yadav, served in the Kumaon Regiment and participated in the 1965 and 1971 Indo-Pakistan wars. Inspired by his family's legacy, Yogendra Singh Yadav joined the Indian Army at the remarkably young age of 16 years and five months. He was enlisted with the 18 Grenadiers, an infantry regiment known for its brave soldiers.**

###### **The Kargil War and the Battle of Tiger Hill:** 

**In 1999, the Kargil War erupted, and Grenadier Yogendra Singh Yadav's unit, the 18 Grenadiers, was deployed to the Drass sector. One of the most crucial and challenging objectives was to capture Tiger Hill, a strategically vital peak occupied by Pakistani intruders. The peak, situated at an altitude of 16,500 feet, had a steep, snow-covered, and rocky cliff face, making it an extremely formidable stronghold. On the night of July 3/4, 1999, Grenadier Yogendra Singh Yadav was part of the Ghatak (commando) Platoon tasked with capturing three strategic bunkers on Tiger Hill. He volunteered to lead the assault, climbing the sheer cliff face to fix ropes for his team. Halfway up the climb, an enemy bunker unleashed intense machine gun and rocket fire, killing the platoon commander and two other**

###### **Param Vir Chakra and Later Career:** 

**Initially, the Param Vir Chakra was announced for Yogendra Singh Yadav posthumously, as it was believed he had succumbed to his injuries. However, it was later discovered that he was recuperating in a military hospital, and it was a namesake who had been martyred in the mission. He thus became one of the rare living recipients of the Param Vir Chakra. His PVC citation on the official Indian Army website commends his "most conspicuous courage, indomitable gallantry, grit and determination under extreme adverse circumstances." After the Kargil War, Yogendra Singh Yadav continued to serve in the Indian Army. He was conferred the honorary rank of Captain by the President of India on Independence Day 2021 and retired from service on December 31, 2021, as an Honorary Captain.
