Assam CM Himanta Biswa Sarma has announced plans to implement the Immigrants (Expulsion from Assam) Act, 1950, enabling direct removal of illegal immigrants— especially those from Bangladesh—without court intervention. 

###### ⦁ **<u>What is the Immigrants (Expulsion from Assam) Act, 1950?</u>** 

This post-Partition legislation empowers the Centre to order the expulsion of persons whose stay in Assam may pose a threat to public order. It was enacted in the wake of massive migration during and after the 1947 Partition. 

- ⦁ **<u>Assam’s Unique Legal Landscape on Migration</u>** 

Unlike other Indian states, Assam has a distinct legal and historical context regarding migration—ranging from the Assam Accord of 1985 to the National Register of Citizens (NRC)—making it a key case study for understanding demographic policy, federalism, and centre-state relations.

###### ⦁ **<u>Current Landscape of the Gig Economy</u>** 

India’s gig economy, once limited to ride-sharing and food delivery, has expanded into diverse sectors like healthcare, education, digital content creation, and professional consulting. In 2020, around 3 million workers were employed by 11 major platforms. This figure is expected to grow to 23 million by 2030—approximately 7% of India’s non-agricultural workforce. 

###### ⦁ **<u>Projected Growth and LongTerm Impact</u>** 

A study by the VV Giri National Labour Institute, based on NITI Aayog's 2022 data, forecasts a significant surge in gig employment. By 2047, the gig and platform workforce is projected to reach 62 million, accounting for about 15% of the total non-farm employment in India—indicating a major shift in India’s labour structure. 

###### **<u>Note-: What is the Gig Economy?</u>** 

The gig economy refers to a labour market where people work on short-term jobs or projects—often through digital platforms—instead of traditional, permanent jobs. Gig workers are usually freelancers or contract-based employees who get paid per task, ride, delivery, or service, depending on their field.

Russia and Ukraine concluded brief peace talks in Türkiye on Monday, facilitated by Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan. While no breakthrough was achieved, both sides agreed to exchange documents and prepare for another prisoner swap, including 6,000 soldiers’ bodies. Talks followed major drone strikes by Ukraine on Russian airbases. The Ukrainian and Russian delegations were led by Defence Minister Rustem Umerov and Putin aide Vladimir Medinsky, respectively.
